Make it easier to use. Then make it beautiful.

Read the complete research

Recommendation: Everyday home + the shared two-pane settings design. This combines familiar iPhone controls, a landscape iPad structure, and smart-display sizing. These are design hypotheses to validate with you, not results from user testing.

Establish one hierarchy

Glanceable time, weather, and next event. Six favorites. Full apps and tools live one tap away. The dashboard is a starting point rather than a collection of every available feature.

Apple: layoutNN/g: dashboards

Keep the iPhone familiarity

Grouped rows, trailing values, switches, and clear disclosure arrows. Eight understandable destinations replace a long feature-by-feature sidebar. Keep deeper settings accessible through search and Advanced.

Apple: settingsNN/g: progressive disclosure

Use glass with a purpose

Glass belongs on the floating navigation and transient controls. Reading surfaces use stable, sufficiently opaque backgrounds. The Android 10 implementation should use inexpensive tints instead of continuous live blur.

Apple: materials

Design for an arm’s length

Android’s floor is 48dp; Amazon recommends 68dp targets for hub devices. Prototype rows are generously sized; final dp, text size, and spacing must be checked on the actual Portal and at room distance.

Amazon: smart-display controlsAndroid: accessible defaults

Remove the guesswork

Visible Customize and Done actions, a Move button alongside dragging, search results that open the relevant setting, connected-state summaries, and errors beside the affected content.

NN/g: recognitionApple: feedback

Keep navigation predictable

Use a stable sidebar and detail pane on wide displays, a back stack on compact displays, and a clear focus indicator for TV remotes. Essential actions never depend on long press or hover.

Sources reviewed

Apple HIG: accessibility, motion, searching. Amazon: smart-display type and spacing. Home Assistant: sections and contextual visibility. W3C: contrast and target sizes.
